# Field Tool 005 — The Asymmetry Check

### A compact decision tool for designing favorable exposure under uncertainty

> **You cannot control the cards. You can design the bet.**

The **Asymmetry Check** is a pre-decision tool for situations where outcomes are uncertain but exposure can still be shaped.

Its purpose is not to predict the future.

It is to ask whether the decision has been structured so that the downside is survivable, the odds can be improved, future choices remain available, and the potential upside justifies the exposure.

## The Core Model

> # FLOOR → ODDS → OPTIONS → UPSIDE → EVIDENCE

### 1. FLOOR
**What can I lose?**

Start with exposure rather than excitement.

Ask:

- What is the maximum credible downside?
- Can I survive it?
- Is the decision reversible?
- Could one bad outcome remove me from the game?
- What must be protected before I pursue the upside?

> **Protect the floor before reaching for the ceiling.**

### 2. ODDS
**Can I improve the probability of a favorable outcome?**

Do not ask only whether something *can* work.

Ask what changes the odds:

- better information;
- more skill;
- stronger positioning;
- smaller experiments;
- better timing;
- incentives;
- base rates;
- repeated attempts;
- feedback.

> **Uncertainty is not the same as helplessness.**

### 3. OPTIONS
**What choices remain after I act?**

Optionality has value when uncertainty is high.

Ask:

- Does this decision preserve future choices?
- Can I test before committing?
- Can I exit?
- Can I scale after evidence appears?
- Can I delay an irreversible decision until I know more?
- Does this move create options—or consume them?

> **Prefer reversible decisions when information is still arriving.**

### 4. UPSIDE
**Does being right matter enough?**

A favorable bet does not merely have limited downside.

It should offer meaningful upside relative to the exposure.

Ask:

- What happens if this works?
- Is the gain materially larger than the bounded loss?
- Can success compound?
- Can the upside repeat?
- Does this create new opportunities beyond the immediate payoff?

### 5. EVIDENCE
**What would make me update?**

A decision becomes dangerous when commitment prevents learning.

Before acting, identify:

- evidence that would strengthen the position;
- evidence that would weaken it;
- a stop condition;
- a scale condition;
- the next review point.

> **Decide what would change your mind before the outcome gives you a reason to defend your ego.**

## The Four-Question Version

When time is limited, reduce the tool to four questions:

> **What can I lose?**  
> **Can I improve the odds?**  
> **What options remain?**  
> **Is the upside asymmetric?**

Then add one discipline:

> **What evidence would make me change the position?**

## Avoid Outcome Bias

A good decision can produce a bad outcome.

A poor decision can produce a good outcome.

Therefore:

> **Judge the quality of the decision separately from the quality of the outcome.**

After the result, return to what was knowable **at the time the decision was made**.

## Position Sizing

The Asymmetry Check is not only about whether to act.

It is also about **how much to expose**.

When uncertainty is high but the potential upside is attractive, a smaller position may preserve participation while limiting ruin.

The useful question may therefore be:

> **Not “Should I bet?” but “How much should I expose before I have more evidence?”**

## The Anti-Ruin Test

Before accepting an attractive upside, ask:

**If this goes badly, can I still make the next decision?**

If the answer is no, the downside may be too large regardless of the potential reward.

> **Survival preserves optionality.**

## The Standard

The goal is not to eliminate uncertainty.

The goal is to avoid needing certainty before acting intelligently.

> **Protect the floor. Improve the odds. Preserve optionality. Position for upside. Update from evidence.**

Or more simply:

> # HEADS I WIN A LOT. TAILS I LOSE A LITTLE.

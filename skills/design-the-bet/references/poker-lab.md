# Poker as a Decision Laboratory

Poker is useful because it makes uncertainty visible.

Players act with incomplete information. Skill and luck coexist. Decisions repeat. Feedback is fast but noisy.

## Transferable concepts

### Position
Information can become more valuable when others act first.

### Expected value
A decision can be favorable across repeated trials even when a particular trial loses.

### Risk of ruin
A theoretically positive opportunity can still be destructive if exposure is too large.

### Outcome bias
Winning does not prove the decision was good. Losing does not prove it was bad.

### Emotional regulation
Decision quality can degrade after wins, losses, frustration, fear, or overconfidence.

### Repetition
A small edge becomes more meaningful when it can be exercised repeatedly without catastrophic exposure.

## Boundary

Poker is an analogy and laboratory, not a universal model. Do not translate game assumptions into real-world domains where stakes, ethics, dependencies, or probabilities differ materially.

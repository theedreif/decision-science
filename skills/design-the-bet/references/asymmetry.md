# Asymmetry

## Core idea

An asymmetric position has a different shape on the downside than on the upside.

The aim is not to eliminate uncertainty. It is to structure exposure so that:

- downside is bounded or survivable;
- ruin is avoided;
- useful learning can occur;
- upside is meaningful if the thesis is right.

## Questions

1. What is the credible downside?
2. Is any loss irreversible?
3. What would constitute ruin?
4. Can position size be reduced?
5. Can the commitment be staged?
6. What is the plausible upside?
7. Is the upside repeatable or scalable?
8. Are losses and gains correlated with other exposures?
9. Does this decision create second-order opportunities?
10. Is the apparent asymmetry real or based on optimistic assumptions?

## Principle

**Heads I win a lot. Tails I lose a little** is an aspiration for position design, not a claim that every uncertain choice can be made safe.

Protect the floor first.

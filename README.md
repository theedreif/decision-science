## The Asymmetrical Model

> # PROTECT THE FLOOR → IMPROVE THE ODDS → PRESERVE OPTIONALITY → POSITION FOR UPSIDE

The objective is not certainty.

It is to design situations where being wrong is survivable and being right matters.

Or, more compactly:

> **Heads I win a lot. Tails I lose a little.**

## Four Questions

### 1. What can I lose?
Before considering upside, understand exposure, ruin, reversibility, and the floor.

### 2. Can I improve the odds?
Look for information, skill, positioning, incentives, base rates, and repeated opportunities.

### 3. What options remain?
Prefer decisions that preserve future choices when uncertainty is high.

### 4. Is the upside asymmetric?
Seek situations where potential gain is meaningfully larger than bounded downside.

## Poker as a Decision Laboratory

Poker makes uncertainty visible.

You act with incomplete information. Outcomes contain both skill and luck. Good decisions can lose. Bad decisions can win. Feedback is immediate but noisy.

That makes poker useful for studying:

- probability and expected value;
- risk and ruin;
- position and information;
- cognitive bias;
- emotional regulation;
- outcome bias;
- calibration;
- optionality;
- repeated decision-making.

The lesson extends beyond the table:

> **Judge the quality of the decision separately from the quality of the outcome.**
